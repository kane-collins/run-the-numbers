The Squeeze: Rents, Prices and Pay

Over a decade, the cost of a roof has quietly outrun the money coming in.

By Kane · 2 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Everyone has a feeling about how expensive housing has become. Feelings are hard to argue with, and harder to compare. So let's put three numbers on the same page and scroll through them together.

Every line below is indexed so that 2015 equals 100. If a line reaches 150, that thing is 50% more expensive than it was at the start.

Start with pay

Wages have risen steadily, but slowly. Over ten years, the typical pay packet is up about a third.

Now add house prices

Prices started pulling away around 2020 and haven't looked back, rising far faster than the money used to buy them.

And rents

Renters were hit hardest. Rents are the steepest line on the chart, and they never get a breather.

The squeeze

That shaded gap between rents and wages is the squeeze: every year, a bigger share of the pay packet goes on keeping a roof overhead.

What it means

When one cost grows nearly three times faster than income, something has to give. Savings stretch thinner, moving becomes harder, and deposits slip further out of reach.
